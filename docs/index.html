Drag this link to your bookmarks bar, then click it on a page with a video: Measure flicker

It measures the largest video on the page for 4 seconds. Many sites (YouTube, Netflix and other streaming services) do not let scripts read their video pixels, so it will tell you it cannot measure there. Downloaded clips and your own files always work.

What the number means

FlickerScope tracks the brightness of the whole frame and of 16 regions over time, removes slow changes with a 0.3 s moving average, and reports the IEEE 1789 Percent Flicker of what is left: 100 × (high − low) / (high + low), using the 95th and 5th percentiles. The headline is the largest of the whole-frame value, the value most of the picture shares, and half of the strongest steady single-region value. Hard cuts and single flashes are left out.

Under 2 % reads as none, under 5 % as slight, under 12 % as visible, and above that as strong. Compression and fast motion can add a percent or two.
